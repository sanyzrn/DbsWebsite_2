Performance evaluation is not an annual form. It is a chain of criteria design, evidence
capture, scoring, review, approval, and analysis. When each link lives in a separate file or
message thread, HR is left with three critical questions: where did this score come from, who
changed it, and how can the decision be defended?

NexaHR started with that problem: turning a scattered, manual process into a workspace where
each evaluation stage, its owner, and its decision history are clear. The goal was not simply
to replace an Excel form. It was to create one trustworthy source of truth for HR decisions.

## Why spreadsheets reach a ceiling

Spreadsheets are excellent for a first pass: fast, familiar, and useful for testing a form.
But as employees, managers, evaluation cycles, and criteria multiply, versions spread and the
business logic gets buried in formulas and files. The result is usually the work teams were
trying to avoid: manual follow-up, ambiguous access, and reports that need another round of
checking.

When evaluation affects development, feedback, or HR decisions, being “digital” is not enough.
The system has to preserve the decision path, control access precisely, and give the team an
understandable outcome.

## What NexaHR makes structured

NexaHR is a performance-evaluation platform designed for multi-stage organizational workflows.
HR can define cycles and criteria, add people and evaluators through a controlled process, and
see the state of every stage from one place. Scores keep their context and feedback, so the
output is more than a number.

The project uses FastAPI for services, PostgreSQL for structured data, and React for an interface
that keeps the work visible. Those choices are not there to look modern. They address real HR
needs: distinct roles, concurrent users, searchable data, and a workflow that can grow.

## Three rules that turn a form into a system

**Access with an organizational owner.** Entry to the evaluation cycle and permissions are
managed through controlled HR assignment. It stays clear who can act, in which role, and at
which stage.

**Scores with evidence.** Any non-neutral score requires at least fifteen words of explanation.
That small rule turns an isolated number into feedback that can still be understood during later
review.

**Auditable rules.** For defined roles, supervisor logic is applied explicitly. For example,
when a job title is exactly “Manager,” the unit-supervisor status locks automatically. The key
decision is that rules live in the system—not only in someone’s memory—and can be explained.

## Current project status and what comes next

NexaHR is an in-progress product. Its core evaluation flow, data model, roles, and main rules
have been designed and implemented. The next phase focuses on completing the operational
experience, management reporting, and readiness for wider organizational deployment. That
clarity matters: the product is not making vague promises; it is solving a real performance
evaluation problem step by step.

If your organization still relies on files, manual follow-up, and verbal decisions to complete
reviews, NexaHR shows how the same process can become a clear, extensible, decision-ready system.
