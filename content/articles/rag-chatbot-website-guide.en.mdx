A general chatbot can make for a compelling conversation, but it has a problem on a business
website: its answer may not be grounded in your real services, pricing, policies, or content.
That is where RAG matters. It is an approach that grounds answers by retrieving relevant
information from an approved business knowledge base.

In simple terms, before creating an answer, the system searches your permitted content and gives
the relevant pieces to the model. Instead of guessing, the chatbot can keep the conversation close
to information you have published and approved.

## When is RAG the right choice?

If your site receives repeated questions about services, products, engagement steps, documentation,
or policies, RAG can be a strong starting point. The goal should not be to eliminate people. It is
to answer simple questions faster and route complex questions to the right person.

For a store, that can mean product discovery and common answers. For a service company, it can mean
early visitor guidance and lead capture. For a SaaS product, it can point users to documentation and
reduce repeated support work. The value appears when it connects to a real sales or service flow.

## Five principles for a chatbot people can use

**Approved, bounded knowledge.** Not every page is a good answer source. Start with current,
accurate content that has a clear owner: FAQs, service pages, catalog data, or documentation.

**Clear answer boundaries.** The chatbot must know when it does not know. Rather than inventing a
likely answer, it should hand the question to a contact form, support teammate, or sales advisor.

**A voice aligned with the brand.** Short, clear, useful answers generally beat long performative
ones. Tone and calls to action should match the brand’s actual language and the visitor’s decision
stage.

**Consent-based lead capture.** If the conversation becomes a consultation request, collect contact
details transparently and only with the visitor’s consent. A chatbot should start a better
relationship, not block one.

**Measure before and after.** Track conversations, successful routes to the right page, escalated
requests, and user feedback. The important measure is not message count—it is whether visitors
reach the right answer or action faster.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Start with twenty real questions">
  Review support tickets, social messages, and sales calls. If you can find twenty repeated
  questions, you have the best brief for the chatbot’s first version.
</Callout>

## Why a custom plugin can be the better fit

For a WordPress website, a custom plugin can align the chatbot with content structure, forms,
WooCommerce, or an existing dashboard without handing the brand experience to a separate,
generic tool. Capabilities can match the project goal precisely: answering questions, qualifying
leads, guiding purchase, or connecting to internal knowledge.

NexaChat is an in-progress WordPress plugin built around that approach, with RAG integration,
WooCommerce, and site data structures. It is both a useful customer-facing assistant and an
example of how a custom plugin can become part of a brand’s digital experience instead of another
generic widget.

A successful RAG chatbot does not produce strange answers. It shortens the visitor’s path, brings
the right information at the right time, and hands the conversation to a person when needed.
